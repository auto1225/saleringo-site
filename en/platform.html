What is actually running

Five parts. The fourth one
is the one nobody else has.

Most AI receptionists are the first three boxes. They hear you, they work out what you meant, they answer from a document. Then they stop. The fourth box is what turns a conversation into work, and the fifth is what makes that work findable on Monday.

01It hears the call

Speech becomes text you can search, with the caller's own words kept. Speech recognition.

02It works out what they want

Date, budget, purpose, how urgent, and whether a person is needed — pulled out and ranked. Language understanding.

03It answers from your facts only

Your prices, your booking rules, your FAQ, and your deny list. Nothing else is in scope. Retrieval-grounded answering.

04It turns the conversation into work

A booking on the calendar, a quote drafted, a follow-up scheduled, a person paged. Workflow engine.

05It files the customer

Summary, conditions, sentiment, where they came from and what happens next — on one card. CRM sync.

The list of things it is not allowed to say

Trust in an AI receptionist is not a feeling, it is a file. Alongside the knowledge base sits a deny list: subjects on which the AI must hand over rather than answer, however confidently it could. You write it, you can count it, and you can change it at 11pm without asking us.

Typical starting set — you edit it before go-live:

Discounts not in your price list Refunds and cancellations Anything clinical or diagnostic Legal advice Personal data about another customer Contract terms and liability Dates your calendar has not confirmed Promises about a result
