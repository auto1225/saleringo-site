Confident handoff

When a conversation goes to a human

A meaningful minority of conversations should reach your team, by design. We are not going to put a fraction on it — the only honest one is the one your own logs produce after a month. What we can tell you exactly is when it happens. These are the five triggers.

CASE 01It is not sure enough

No approved fact matches the question closely enough. Instead of guessing, the AI hands over.

→ Not certain enough — handing to your team
CASE 02Knowledge conflict

Two approved facts disagree — say, two prices for one package. Answering pauses on that topic until you resolve it.

→ Paused — two prices found for one package
CASE 03Expired facts

The only relevant fact is past its expiry date. Last season's prices and old hours never reach a customer.

→ Fact expired — a person will confirm
CASE 04Sensitive intent

Medical, legal, safety, or complaint territory goes to a person — however sure the AI is.

→ This needs a person — handing to staff
CASE 05Commitment language

"Can you guarantee the date?" Booking confirmations, dates, and contract terms always go to a person.

→ Date confirmation needs a person

Honest limits

What this does not do

A product that states its limits out loud is a product you can trust on everything else.

It fills in everything it can prove — and leaves the signature to you.

It does not make the AI perfect.

It makes the AI accountable to you. Facts can still be wrong if what you approved was wrong — but you'll always know which fact was used.

It does not replace your team.

A hand-off is the design, not a shortfall — and the five triggers above decide when one happens, not a quota we set.

It does not sign contracts.

Saleringo will not confirm a booking, promise a date, or agree to terms on your behalf. Those always go to a person.

You have seen where it stops

The deny list is a file you edit, not a promise we make. Every trade gets a different one — a dental boundary and a venue boundary are not the same shape.
