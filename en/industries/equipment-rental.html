FAQ

Hire desks ask us

Does it connect to our hire system?

Not directly, and we will not pretend otherwise. Today Saleringo syncs reservations to Google or Outlook Calendar, exports customer and enquiry records as CSV, and pushes events through Zapier. A two-way link to a hire management system — inspHire, MCS, Syrinx, Point of Rental — is on the roadmap and founding customers vote on the build order. Until one exists, availability is treated as unknown and every enquiry is reserved for your hire desk to confirm.

What stops it double-hiring a machine?

It has no authority to confirm. The AI can quote a rate, read a policy and reserve an enquiry against a date range and a depot, but a machine is not on hire until someone at your desk says it is. Availability in this trade changes hourly, which is exactly why it holds rather than promises. Read the Security & data page for the full rule set.

Will it quote the whole cost, or just the day rate?

The whole cost, from your own rate card: the day, weekend, week or long-hire rate for the dates asked for, delivery and collection each way, fuel, consumables and the damage waiver. A caller told only the day rate finds the rest out at the counter, and that is the argument nobody wants to have.

Can it take a deposit or a card payment?

It never touches card details. Once your hire desk confirms, the AI can send your existing deposit or payment link and chase it if it goes unpaid. It cannot reduce a deposit, waive one, or agree terms for a first-time cash customer — collecting the money stays with the tools you already trust, and the decisions stay with your team.

Can it tell a customer whether they're allowed to operate something?

No, and this one is a flat refusal rather than a setting. Some plant needs a card, a licence or a certificate; some equipment needs a trained operator. The AI reads back what your hire terms require and records what the customer says they hold. Whether that person is competent to run that machine is decided by your team, at the counter, with the card in hand. It also gives no operating, safety or method-statement instructions of any kind.
