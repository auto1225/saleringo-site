and what it will not do.

The CRM behind the call, the fields and stages this trade actually works in, and the systems it hands to. The argument for any of it is on the Senior living & care page.

Most AI receptionists hand you a transcript.
This one hands you an enquiry already written up.

A polite answer is not a system. The other half of Saleringo is a CRM built around the enquiry — who is calling and whether they may discuss the person, where that person is tonight, what the family says they are looking for, the date they are working to. All of it moves through the stages your manager already works in.

Nine fields, and two of them stop the call

A generic CRM asks what the customer wants.
This one asks who is allowed to tell you.

  • →Relationship and consent to discuss come first. The caller is almost never the person who would move in. Who they are, and whether the person concerned knows they are ringing, is established before any detail about that person is written down.
  • →Where the person is tonight routes everything. At home, on a ward, in another home, in temporary respite — it decides who else has to be spoken to and how fast the assessment has to happen.
  • →What the family says they need is stored as their words. "He gets confused in the evenings" is recorded as that sentence. It is never converted into a care band, a diagnosis or a recommendation by the AI.
  • →The discharge date is a scheduling rule, not a note. A stated date pulls the show-round and the assessment to the first slots that land before it, and marks the file so the manager sees it first.
  • →Two fields end the script. A safeguarding concern and a described medical emergency are not fields that get filled in and moved on from. Either one stops the conversation and starts dialling.

Provisional until a manager says otherwise —
and one flag that ends the script.

The first field is the caller, not the enquiry: who they are to the person, and whether consent was asked. Beside it, where the person is tonight and the family’s own words. A discharge date pulls show-round and assessment forward. The AI confirms nothing; only the safeguarding lead closes a concern flag, by phone, during the call.

What the calendar enforces
  1. A stated discharge date pulls the show-round and the assessment to the first slots that land before it, not the next free ones.
  2. Nothing is confirmed by the AI. Slots are held as provisional and said out loud as provisional until your manager confirms.
  3. A concern-flagged record cannot be closed by the system. Only a named person closes it.
What a figure is read from
  • The weekly rate for the care type asked about
  • Home care hourly rates and minimum call length
  • Respite terms and minimum stay, as published
  • Anything about funding — not read at all
Who ends up with the work

The registered manager takes the show-round and the assessment. The on-duty coordinator takes the rota calls. The safeguarding lead takes the concern, by phone, during the call.

Where it stops

Clinical advice, care-level decisions, funding, promises of a place or a date, and any concern about a person’s safety. Every one of those is a person.

What you judge it on after ninety days
Enquiries answered on the first ringShow-rounds booked out of hoursFee questions answered in fullMinutes from concern raised to a named person answering

Fee schedule, escalation list and appointment lengths come from your setup; the sample card fields and concern flag are real.Enquiries answered, show-rounds booked by morning, a measurable gap between concern spoken and person hearing it. A registered manager tracks all three — the only change is who was awake when the phone rang.

This is the manifest, not the argument. Why any of it matters — the call that arrives at night, what it costs to miss, what the AI refuses to say — is on the page this came from.
