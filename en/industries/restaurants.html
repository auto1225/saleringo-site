Where the AI stops talking

Capturing an allergy
is allowed. Answering one
is not.

That sentence is the whole boundary, and it is not a style choice. In the European Union and the United Kingdom, Regulation 1169/2011 and Natasha's Law make allergen information a legal duty with criminal liability attached to getting it wrong; comparable obligations exist in the United States and elsewhere. An answering system that says "should be fine" is not a bad feature — it is a prosecution waiting for a date. So this one writes the allergy down and hands it to the pass.

Never answered "Is this dish safe for her?"

Recorded in the caller's own words, attached to the booking, booking marked unconfirmed until the kitchen replies, question sent to whoever you nominate that night. No reassurance in any wording, including the gentle ones.

Never touched A card number, over the phone

It states the deposit amount and the cancellation terms as written and sends your own payment link. It does not read back, repeat or store a card, and it will not hold a peak date on a promise of one later.

Hard boundary It will not overbook

It books against the real floor plan and the real turn time. If 8 PM on a Saturday does not exist it says so, offers the sittings that do, and adds the guest to the waitlist. A table it invents is a table your host apologises for.

STEP 01Capture verbatimThe allergy or dietary requirement goes onto the record in the guest's own words — not summarised, not categorised.
STEP 02Stop sellingNo reassurance, no menu recommendation, no "we can work around it". The booking is marked unconfirmed on that point alone.
STEP 03Ask the kitchenThe question reaches the head chef or whoever you nominate, that night, with the covers and the date attached.
STEP 04A person confirmsThe booking becomes confirmed when a human answers, and the answer is recorded next to the question.

Every one of these is a rule you approve before it goes live, and none of them is a switch someone can flip to close a booking faster. How answers get approved →

The fields a restaurant actually needs

A generic CRM asks for a name.
This one asks for the turn time.

  • →Peak dates carry their own minimum. A December Friday is not a February Tuesday, and the quote knows the difference before the caller does. Nothing is improvised and nothing is discounted.
  • →The allergen field is capture-only, with a gate behind it. It writes the words down and marks the booking unconfirmed until a chef answers. It is the only field in this pack that can block a confirmation on its own.
  • →Turn time and sitting, not "a time slot". On a two-sitting night the question is not whether 20:00 is free — it is whether the table can be held that long. That is the field that stops an overbooking.
  • →Held is not confirmed. The date is held against your deposit rule and released on your clock, so a peak Friday is never quietly sitting on a maybe.
See the pipeline it fills
Two sites. Three. A group.

The riverside room does not quote
the high-street site's minimum.

Two rooms in the same group almost never share a function sheet, and the fastest way to lose a December booking is to quote the other one's minimum. Each site is set up as its own restaurant inside one account.

01Its own function sheet and its own minimums

Room hire, peak and off-peak minimum spend, per-head menus and corkage are held per site. A caller about one room never hears another room's figure.

02Its own floor plan and sittings

Turn time, sitting structure and the largest party the room can physically hold are that site's own. One site running two sittings does not make the other one do the same.

03Each manager sees only their own room

A general manager opens the board and sees their inquiries, their held dates, their kitchen questions — not the whole group's.

FAQ

Rooms like yours ask us

A guest says one of the party has a severe nut allergy. What exactly happens?

It records the allergy in the guest's own words, attaches it to the booking, marks the booking unconfirmed pending the kitchen, and sends the question to whoever you nominate that night. It does not say the set menu is fine, it does not say the kitchen is careful, and it does not say a dish is nut free. Capturing an allergy is allowed; answering one is not.

Will it take a deposit or a card over the phone?

No. It states your deposit rule and your cancellation terms as written and sends your own payment link. It never reads back, repeats or records a card number, and it will not hold a date on a promise that a card will be given later.

Can it overbook if the caller pushes?

No. It books against the real floor plan and the real turn time. If 8 PM on a Saturday does not exist, it says so, offers the sittings that do, and adds the guest to the waitlist. A table it invents is a table your host has to apologise for.

Reservations mostly come in while you are open. Is this really an after-hours product?

Reservations do skew toward opening hours, and we are not going to claim otherwise. This page is built around the function and catering inquiry, which is the one that arrives at 10:40 PM from somebody who has just finished their own shift — and around the phone that rings while the floor is full and nobody can reach it.

How does it quote a private room?

From your function sheet, line by line: the room hire fee, the minimum spend for that date and service, and the per-head set menu you approved. Peak dates carry their own minimum. It does not improvise a package and it does not discount.

Do you integrate with OpenTable, Resy or SevenRooms?

Not directly, and we will not pretend otherwise. Bookings and function inquiries reach them today through Zapier, a direct feed or CSV export, and founding-cohort members vote on the build order. We will not name a month we cannot stand behind.

Does it answer in the guest's language?

Yes, in the languages you have approved. It detects the language from the first sentence and answers from wording reviewed in that language, not machine-translated at the moment of speaking. The function sheet still reaches your office in English.
