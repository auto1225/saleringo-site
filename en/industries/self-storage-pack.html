The fields a storage site actually needs

A generic CRM asks for a name.
This one asks what the sofa is like.

  • →The size question, answered honestly. Your own guide applied to what they actually described, and the assumption stated — so nobody turns up on Saturday with furniture that will not go in.
  • →Deposit and insurance in the first price sentence. Both are conditions, not extras. The argument at the counter on move-in day is the one this removes.
  • →Access hours kept apart from office hours. Sixteen-hour gate access does not mean sixteen hours of staff, and the caller who learns that on the phone is not the one shouting at a keypad on Sunday.
  • →Held at a size, never at a unit number. It cannot see which units are free. So it does not pretend to.

A size and a van time —
and the door number it never says.

A storage enquiry is recorded in the caller’s own words — what they are storing, exactly as they said it — then matched to a size with the assumption written next to it: 75 sq ft, and why. The move-in date and the van time decide everything else. No unit number is ever spoken: a size is quoted and the enquiry is held.

It will not waive a deposit or a notice period, advise on insurance, or say one word about another customer’s unit.

What the answer set enforces
  1. No unit number is ever spoken. A size is quoted and the enquiry is held.
  2. Deposit and mandatory cover are said in the same breath as the monthly fee, never after it.
  3. An item on the prohibited list ends the sale of that item, not the call — and the item is written down.
What a quote is built from
  • Monthly fee for the matched size
  • Promotion as published, with its minimum term
  • Refundable deposit
  • Cover at the declared value
  • Total on the day, and the monthly after it
Who ends up with the work

The site manager opens the morning with a size, a date and a van time, and one decision to make: which door.

Where it stops

Naming a free unit, waiving a deposit or a notice period, agreeing to store something on your list, advising on insurance, or saying one word about another customer's unit.
