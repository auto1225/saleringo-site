Urgency is not a tone of voice

Everyone who rings you
sounds like an emergency.
Three of them are.

The caller's distress is real and it is not the input. What sets the slot is the pest, the site and who is standing next to it — and those are three questions, asked the same way every time, by something that is not embarrassed and not in a hurry.

NOT YOUR JOB — SAY SOHoney bees, bats, nesting birds

A swarm hanging off a branch is a beekeeper's call. Bats and their roosts are protected in the UK under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981; in the United States most native birds and their active nests are protected under the Migratory Bird Treaty Act. The AI books none of it.

The tiers, the questions and what counts as today are yours — we write them with you during onboarding and you approve the wording before a caller hears any of it.

Where the AI stops talking

Half the "wasps"
are somebody else's job.

A honey bee swarm is a beekeeper's work, not a treatment, and a firm that sprays one has done something it cannot take back — to the bees and to its own name in that town. So the AI never identifies a species from a description. These limits ship enabled; they are not settings anyone can switch off to hit a booking target.

Straight to a person A sting, a bite, a reaction

Booking stops mid-sentence. The caller hears the wording you approved: a doctor, or the emergency number if breathing or the face is involved, and a vet if it is an animal. It offers no view of its own on whether a swelling is normal, and it does not soften the instruction to keep the call moving.

Straight to a person Anything that might be honey bees

A cluster on a branch, a fence post, a chimney. The AI says the possibility out loud — as a possibility, never as a fact — reads out the beekeeper route your firm supplied, and books nothing. Same for bats and for birds on an active nest.

Hard boundary No species, no DIY, no "safe for"

It will not name the insect from a phone call, will not tell anybody what to buy and spray themselves, and will not say a treatment is safe around a named child, animal or condition. It records who and what is in the property and passes it to the technician who decides on site.

Two vans. Three depots. One owner.

The commercial contract does not
get quoted the domestic nest price.

Domestic and commercial are two businesses sharing a phone number. The quickest way to lose money is a one-nest price read out to a site that needed a contract, a survey and a report.

01Its own line, hours and rate card

The domestic line quotes nests and programmes. The commercial line takes trading name, site, contract status and whether an inspection is booked — and never reads the other card's numbers.

02Its own round and its own technicians

The visit lands on the round that covers that postcode, from the diary that depot actually runs, with the ladder and the equipment the card says it needs.

03Each depot sees only its own board

A depot manager opens the pipeline and sees their calls, their visits, their surveys and their overdue reports — not the whole company's.

04You see all of them, side by side

One report, every depot in the same row: calls answered, visits booked, surveys converted, follow-ups attended, reports issued. Where the vans are earning, and where they are driving.

FAQ

Firms like yours ask us

A caller says someone has been stung and it is swelling. What exactly happens?

Booking stops. It reads the wording you approved: a reaction to a sting is a question for a doctor — or your local emergency number if breathing or the face is involved — and for an animal, a vet. It gives no opinion of its own and does not soften the instruction to keep the call moving. The call is flagged so a person sees it first thing.

Can it tell a wasp from a bee over the phone?

No, and it will not pretend to. A description down a phone line is not an identification, and getting it wrong matters: a honey bee swarm is a beekeeper's job, not a treatment.

It records the caller's own words, books an unconfirmed stinging insect, asks for a photo where the channel allows, and leaves the identification to the technician. If the description points at honey bees, it says so as a possibility — never a fact — and offers the beekeeper route your firm supplied.

Do you integrate with PestPac, ServSuite or Pocomos?

Not directly, and we are not going to imply otherwise. Jobs reach them today through Zapier or CSV export, and every call also lands in Saleringo's own inbox and CRM. Direct field-service integrations are in build, and founding-cohort members vote on the order. We will not name a month we cannot stand behind.

Can it quote a bedbug or a rodent job over the phone?

It quotes exactly what you put on the rate card — a nest price, a survey fee, a programme price, an out-of-hours surcharge. It will not price an infestation nobody has seen. Bedbugs and commercial rodent work start with a survey: the number depends on rooms, harbourage and access, and a figure guessed on the phone gets argued on the doorstep.

Can I start with evenings and weekends only?

That is how most firms begin, and wasp season is the usual reason. Point the line at us from 5 PM and across the weekend, read a week of transcripts, then widen the window when the wording sounds like yours. If you want it off again, that is a forwarding change you make, not a support request.
