The fields a practice actually needs

A generic CRM asks for a name.
This one asks who they want to see.

  • →New-patient intake, finished before they arrive. The form goes out on the channel they used, and the answers land on the record — not in someone's inbox.
  • →Treatment-plan estimates from your own fee schedule. Always a range, always until an exam. Nothing is priced that you have not priced yourself.
  • →No-show and recall follow-up that fires itself. The chair that emptied at 9:00 gets a message by 9:15, and the hygiene recall sets itself the day treatment starts.
  • →A Monday number, not a feeling. New patients, AI-handled rate, chairs filled, plan acceptance — per practitioner and per location, exportable to PDF or Excel.
See the pipeline it fills

Forty minutes or twenty,
and the line it never crosses.

A dental record is built around a tooth and a number: the area in the caller’s own words, and the pain score from 0 to 10 that decides today from this week. New names get the 40-minute slot and returning ones get 20 — the AI cannot book the short slot for a new patient, and two chairs cannot claim the same hygienist. A quote is the consultation, the exam and X-ray, and the procedure off your fee schedule; insurance is captured, never estimated. Anything clinical — diagnosis, whether it will hurt, whether the tooth has to come out — belongs to the dentist.

What the calendar enforces
  1. New patients get the 40-minute slot, returning get 20 — the AI cannot book the short one for a new name.
  2. Emergency words move the request into today’s buffer slot, not the next free gap.
  3. Two chairs cannot claim the same hygienist at the same time.
