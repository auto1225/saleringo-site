The fields a restaurant actually needs

A generic CRM asks for a name.
This one asks for the turn time.

  • →Peak dates carry their own minimum. A December Friday is not a February Tuesday, and the quote knows the difference before the caller does. Nothing is improvised and nothing is discounted.
  • →The allergen field is capture-only, with a gate behind it. It writes the words down and marks the booking unconfirmed until a chef answers. It is the only field in this pack that can block a confirmation on its own.
  • →Turn time and sitting, not "a time slot". On a two-sitting night the question is not whether 20:00 is free — it is whether the table can be held that long. That is the field that stops an overbooking.
  • →Held is not confirmed. The date is held against your deposit rule and released on your clock, so a peak Friday is never quietly sitting on a maybe.
See the pipeline it fills

Six fields, three rules,
and one answer the kitchen owns.

The Wedding & Venues pack never confirms a date — a wedding is always a conversation with a person. A restaurant is different: it does confirm the table. What it refuses instead is the allergen answer and the overbooking — which is why this is its own pack and not a re-skin of that one. See the Venues pack

What the calendar enforces
  1. An allergy on the record blocks confirmation until a chef answers — the booking stays held, never confirmed.
  2. The floor plan and the turn time are hard limits. A sitting that does not exist is never offered.
  3. A held date runs on your deposit clock and releases itself; it never sits on a maybe over a peak weekend.
What a quote is built from
  • Room hire for that room
  • Minimum spend for that date and service
  • Set menu, per head, at the party size
  • Corkage and approved add-ons only
Who ends up with the work

The manager owns the held date and the deposit; the head chef owns every allergen question before a booking confirms.

Where it stops

Saying a dish is safe, touching a card, or seating a party the floor cannot hold.
