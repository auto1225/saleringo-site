What actually arrives, and where it goes wrong

The group number is
answering for a business
it cannot see.

Whoever picks up your central line is being asked about a store they have never stood in. Different opening hours, a different price list, a different manager, sometimes a different owner. Then the next caller is not a customer at all — they want to buy a territory, and the honest answer to their second question is a regulated one. Most answering services treat all of this as a single inbox. It is two businesses, and running them through one queue is how a group loses walk-ins on Saturday and gets a letter on Monday.

Why two boards and not one

A filter can be removed.
A separate pipeline cannot.

  • →Location is resolved before anything is quoted. The AI does not answer a price question until it knows which site it is answering for — and if the caller is ambiguous, it asks rather than picks.
  • →Candidates never appear in a store's inbox. Your franchisees do not need to know who is asking about a territory near them, and in most groups it is actively unhelpful that they do.
  • →The refusals are structural, not tone. Earnings, territory availability and contract terms are not phrased carefully — they are not answered, and the candidate is handed to your franchise team with the question intact.
  • →One report, every site in the same row. Inquiries, handled without staff, booked, missed during opening hours — per location and combined, exportable to PDF or Excel for the Monday meeting.

FAQ

Franchise groups ask us

Will it ever give a candidate an earnings figure?

No, and not as a setting you could switch on. It quotes the audited disclosure document verbatim and nothing else. Monthly earnings, projected returns, payback periods, "what most operators make" — all refused, with the candidate handed to your franchise team and the question recorded word for word so the callback starts where they left off. Getting this wrong is a legal problem rather than a service one, so it is a structural refusal, not a carefully worded answer.

Can one number serve every location?

Yes, and each location can also keep its own. On a group line the AI resolves which site the caller means before it quotes anything — and if the caller is ambiguous it asks rather than picks. On a site line, that site is already known. Either way the booking lands on that location's calendar, at that location's price, in that location's inbox.

What stops it quoting the wrong site's price?

Ordering. A price only exists once a location has been resolved, because prices are held per site rather than per group. If a location has not been established, the AI will not name a figure — it asks which one you mean. Nothing is averaged across sites and there is no group default price to fall back on.

Who sees what — franchisees, managers, head office?

Each manager opens the pipeline and sees their own site: their inquiries, their bookings, their missed calls, their flagged complaints. The owner or franchisor sees every site in one report with the same columns. The candidate pipeline lives at head office and no location can open it — your franchisees do not need to see who is asking about a territory near them.

What happens when a candidate asks about a territory?

Nothing is promised. The AI says plainly that it cannot tell them a territory is available, captures the area they named and their timeline, sends the information pack, and puts them in front of your franchise team the same evening. There is no hedged version of this answer — an implied territory promise made by software is still a promise your group has to keep.

Does it connect to our franchise or field-management system?

Not directly — whichever one you run, treat it as a bridge. Records reach it through Zapier or a CSV export your team imports, per site and combined, and every conversation also lands in Saleringo's own inbox with bookings on its own scheduler. A bridge means somebody does a step, and if your group cannot absorb that step it is better for both of us that you know before you pay.

Can it handle inquiries that aren't customers at all?

Yes, and this is worth more than it sounds. Supplier, press, careers and landlord inquiries are routed to the destinations you nominate at head office rather than landing in a store's booking queue. Location managers keep an inbox that is only customers, which is the difference between a tool they use and one they mute.

What does it cost across a dozen locations?

The published plans: Start $79/mo covers one location, Grow $249/mo up to three, and Scale from $599/mo up to ten locations. Calendars, Zapier, webhooks and CSV are included; direct syncs are on the roadmap. AI Phone starts at Scale, and voice usage is billed on top from $0.14/min in the US. Locations and channels are separate limits, which catches multi-site buyers out constantly — the pricing page works a real month through in full. Beyond ten locations, ask us on the call.
