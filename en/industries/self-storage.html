Access hours and office hours are two different things, and callers run them together every time. A nine-to-six office is closed for 15 of the day’s 24 hours — and both weekend days after that. Across a full week the phone is answered for 45 of 168 hours. The gate, meanwhile, is open sixteen hours a day — so most of the week your site is trading and nobody is on the line.

Every scenario below is a worked illustration of how the product behaves, built from the size guide, price list and prohibited list a site would load. FAQ

Storage operators ask us

Does it connect to our storage management software?

Not yet, and we will not pretend otherwise. Today Saleringo puts move-in appointments and site visits on Google or Outlook Calendar, exports enquiries as CSV, and pushes events through Zapier. A two-way link to unit inventory — SiteLink, storEDGE, Stora, Space Manager — is on the roadmap and founding customers vote on the order. Until it exists, no unit is ever named on a call.

Can it tell a caller that a unit is free?

No. It can say what a size costs, what the deposit and insurance are, and it can hold the enquiry at that size. It cannot see your unit inventory, so it does not promise unit 214 to anyone. Your manager names the unit. Read the Security & data page for the full rule set.

Insurance is mandatory here. Will it actually say so?

Yes, in the same sentence as the price, before anyone asks. It states that cover is a condition of the licence and quotes the premium at the value the caller declares. What it will not do is advise — it never tells anyone how much cover they need, never reads or interprets a policy, and never says whether a household policy would do instead. Those go to a person.

What happens when someone wants to store something on our prohibited list?

The answer is no, and it does not soften it. Fuel, gas bottles, chemicals, food, plants, anything living, anything illegal — it reads your list as written and refuses to confirm that an item on it can be stored, however the caller reframes the question. The call is logged with the item in the caller's own words so your manager can go through the list with them before the van is loaded.

Someone rings asking about another person's unit. What does it do?

It does not confirm that the unit exists. No balance, no contents, no access times, no gate code, no acknowledgement that the person is a customer — to a relative, a partner, a landlord or anyone else. It takes a name and a number and your manager calls back.
