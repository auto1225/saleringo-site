You get one chance
to be the place she rang back.

Everything a family notices on a show-round was decided deliberately — the light in the lounge, what the corridor smells like, who greets them. The call that decides whether they ever walk through the door usually comes at nine at night, to a line nobody is sitting at, and it goes to the next home on the list.

See how it answers an enquiry  →

A nine-to-six office is closed for 15 of the day’s 24 hours — and both weekend days after that. Across a full week the office phone is staffed for 45 of 168 hours. A working daughter rings after she has got home, fed her own children and read the discharge letter — which is to say, in the other 123.

This is not a statistic we collected. It is what an ordinary working week divides into.

Written by a regulator, not by us

In the UK, the fee question is not
a later conversation. It belongs at first contact.

The Competition and Markets Authority publishes consumer law advice for UK care home providers. It expects information about fees and charges to reach prospective residents and their representatives at first contact — including on the provider’s website. And first contact, for most families, is a phone call made in the evening.

What the advice describes
First contact

Fees and charges sit in the list of upfront information the CMA expects to reach families at the start, not after a visit and not with the contract. That is a description of a conversation, and the conversation happens on the phone.

What that requires of the line
Somebody answering

Published rates on a website do not read themselves out at half past nine. An answered line that reads the band she asked for and sends the full schedule is the mechanism, and it is the one part of this a piece of software can actually take on.

Source: Competition and Markets Authority — Care homes: consumer law advice for providers, published 16 November 2018, updated 8 December 2021. Read the guidance itself; we have not paraphrased it into a claim it does not make, and we have not attached a percentage to it.

One call is an enquiry.

The one path that is not a workflow

If a caller describes someone at risk,
the AI’s only job is to get a person on the line.

01It stops taking details. No further intake questions, no "and what time did that happen", no attempt to establish whether the concern is founded. Establishing that is a trained person’s work.
02It says out loud what it is doing. The caller hears the name of the person being called and why. Nobody is told a manager "will be in touch".
03It dials your escalation list, in order, during the call. Safeguarding lead, then registered manager, then on-call director — real numbers you supply, tried one after another while the caller is still there.
04If the caller describes a medical emergency, it says to call the emergency services. It gives the number for the caller’s country and does not ask them to stay on the line for anything else first.
05The record cannot be closed by the AI. A call marked as a concern stays open, timestamped, with every number tried and who answered, until a named person closes it.

Where it stops

What it will never do.

On this trade the refusals are not a safety feature bolted to a sales tool. They are the product. These boundaries are built in — not configured, not optional, and they do not soften because a ward wants a bed by Friday.

It answers the line. It never makes the judgement.
✕ Give clinical or medication adviceNot about a bruise, a fall, a missed tablet, a change overnight or anything else. It does not hedge it, soften it or offer a general line. If risk is described it names the emergency number and gets a person on the call.
✕ Decide what care someone needsResidential, nursing, dementia care, respite, four visits a day — that follows an assessment by people qualified to make it. The family’s description is kept in their own words and handed over unconverted.
✕ Promise a place, a room or a dateVacancy status is stated as your manager last set it, and nothing beyond that. Provisional is said out loud, every time, including when the caller is under real time pressure.
✕ Say anything about fundingNo eligibility, no means testing, no local authority rate, no top-up or third-party contribution, no continuing healthcare. It reads the fee you published and stops there.
✕ Take a safeguarding concern as a messageA concern is not intake and it is not a ticket. It stops the conversation, names the person being called, dials your escalation list while the caller is still there, and leaves a record only a named person can close.
Guardrails & security  →

A discharge date is
not an assessment.

It is a deadline the family has been handed. The AI treats it as a scheduling constraint and gets your manager in front of it — it never lets the deadline decide the answer.

One product, several jurisdictions

Care regulation is local.
This page is read everywhere.

So it is worth being exact. In every country Saleringo does the same thing: answer the line, read the fee schedule you published, book what your diary allows, and hand anything clinical, financial or safeguarding to a person you named. Registration, inspection and what your fee information must contain are matters for your regulator and your own advisers.

In EnglandAdult social care providers are registered with and inspected by the Care Quality Commission. Nothing here changes your registration, your statement of purpose or your own policies — the AI works inside them and hands off at the edges you set.
Safeguarding routes to people, not to usIn England, section 42 of the Care Act 2014 places the duty to make safeguarding enquiries on the local authority. Your own policy says who reports and when. The AI’s role is narrow and fixed: reach the named person on your list, immediately, and say so.
Everywhere elseNo equivalent regulator or duty is assumed. The AI still reads only the fee schedule you published, still refuses clinical and funding questions, and still escalates a concern to the names and numbers you configured. This is not legal advice and not a compliance certification.

Two homes. Three. A group with a home care arm.

One home's weekly fee
is not another home's.

If you run more than one service, a confident figure from the wrong schedule is worse than no figure at all, and an escalation sent to the wrong manager is worse again. Each service is set up as its own registered entity inside a single account.

01Its own number, hours and fee schedule

Each home answers on its own line with its own weekly rates, its own home care hourly rates and its own vacancy status. A family asking about one service never hears another’s figures.

02Its own escalation list

The safeguarding lead dialled is the one registered for that service tonight — not a head-office number that rings out until Monday.

03Each manager sees only their own service

Questions registered managers ask

Asked by providers like yours.

Will it tell a family what level of care their relative needs?

No. Residential, nursing, dementia care, respite, home visits — that decision follows an assessment by your own staff, and often by the local authority or hospital as well. The AI records what the caller says, in the caller’s words, and books the assessment or the show-round. It never tells anyone their mother needs a nursing bed.

What happens if a caller describes someone at risk?

It stops taking details and gets a person on the line, saying so out loud. Your safeguarding lead and out-of-hours manager are named in the configuration. If nobody answers, it works down the escalation list — emergency services included when a medical emergency is described. A concern is never a morning note, never answered by the AI.

Can it quote our fees?

It reads the weekly or hourly rate you published for the band asked about, exactly as written, and sends the whole schedule. It will not estimate a total, price an unassessed care level, or touch funding — local authority rates, means testing, top-up or third-party contributions, continuing healthcare. Those are answered by a person who knows the case.

How is enquiry information handled?

Each conversation captures who is calling and their relationship, where the person is now, what the family is looking for, stated urgency or discharge date, the slot booked, and any escalation to a named person.

Records are kept separate from every other provider and encrypted in transit and at rest, with retention and deletion controls your organisation operates. Where they live, and every subprocessor that touches them, is listed in Security & data.
