The fields a store actually needs

A generic CRM asks for a name.
This one asks for the order.

  • →What was quoted, and where it came from. Not "answered" — the actual sentence, and the line of your published policy it was read out of. It is the field that makes a dispute short.
  • →What was refused, and how many times it was asked. "Asked twice for a guaranteed date, declined twice" is worth more on a Monday than any satisfaction score.
  • →The language the buyer wrote in. One order record, twelve languages on the front of it. Your team reads every one of them in theirs.
  • →A Monday number. Contacts taken, share closed without a person, out-of-hours share, exceptions handed over, repeat contacts on the same order — exportable to PDF or Excel.
See the pipeline it fills

The last scan, the published window,
and the sentences it will not say.

A support record is worth little without the order number and the exact variant, so size and colourway are fields, not a product name. The published returns window is the window: outside it, the case is gathered and a person rules on it. A carrier’s last scan stays a scan and its estimate stays an estimate — never a delivery date.

Each storefront answers from its own policy, currency and duty terms, never another’s. Five things never happen here: a promised delivery date, an approval outside the published policy, a duty or tax figure, a stock level it cannot read, a card number on any channel.

What the policy enforces
  1. The published window is the window. Outside it, nothing is decided — the case is gathered and a person decides.
  2. A carrier estimate stays an estimate. No sentence turns it into a delivery date.
  3. A storefront's answers come from that storefront's policy, currency and duty terms. Never from another one's.
What an answer is built from
  • The order record — items, variant, address, service
  • The carrier's last scan and its own estimate
  • Your published returns, delivery and refund policy
  • Your stock record — when it can read it, and only then
Who ends up with the work

Whoever runs care gets the exceptions with order, dates, photos and history already attached; whoever packs gets the variant and sizing notes in writing.

Where it stops

Promising a delivery date, approving anything outside the published policy, quoting a duty or tax figure, inventing stock — and taking a card number, on any channel.
