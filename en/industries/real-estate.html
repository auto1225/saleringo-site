Where the AI stops talking

Three questions
it will not answer.

What it is worth. What they would take. What sort of people live there. The first two belong to a valuer and a negotiator; the third belongs to nobody in the office, because answering it at all is how an agency ends up in front of a regulator. These limits ship enabled; they are not settings someone can switch off to warm a lead up.

Never priced No valuation, no range, no "about"

"What's my house worth?" produces a booked market appraisal in a named valuer's diary, never a figure. It will not give a bracket, will not say whether an asking price looks high, and will not read a portal estimate back as if it were the agency's view.

Never negotiated What an offer would be accepted at

It does not say what a vendor or landlord would take, does not hint, and does not disclose another party's offer, position or reason for moving. The question is recorded verbatim against the applicant and handed to the negotiator who is allowed to answer it.

Never steered The same script, in the same order

Every caller on a listing gets the identical question set, and the transcript proves it. It answers nothing about who lives on a road or which street would "suit" someone, and nothing about the caller changes what it offers, in any wording.

STEP 01Confirm the listingAvailable or not, the advertised price or rent, the date it is free — read from your own stock, never reconstructed.
STEP 02Ask the approved setYour questions, your order, every caller. Move date, who is on the tenancy or the purchase, position to proceed.
STEP 03Book a real slotOnly where the negotiator's diary and the occupier's notice period both allow it. If nothing fits, it says so.
STEP 04Leave the recordTime of enquiry, the questions as asked, the fee facts as stated, and anything it refused — on one applicant record.

The fields a front desk actually needs

A generic CRM asks for a name.
This one asks when their tenancy ends.

  • →The approved question set, in order. Not a checklist the AI works through in whatever order the conversation wanders — a fixed sequence you sign off, identical for every caller, and visible as such in the transcript. It is the field that makes the rest of this page defensible.
  • →Position to proceed — captured, never scored. Chain-free, first-time buyer, mortgage in principle held or not, when the current tenancy ends. Written down as the caller said it. The AI does not rank applicants and does not decide who gets offered which slot.
  • →What was said about money, word for word. Holding deposits, referencing criteria and what a tenant can be charged are the first thing an applicant disputes later. The record shows what was stated, when, and to whom.
  • →Everything it refused. A separate field, not a line lost in a transcript — because "would they take less" is the call you most want a human ringing back about, and it should be the first thing on the board.
See the pipeline it fills
Two branches. Nine. Sales on one line, lettings on the other.

One branch's diary never
fills another branch's Saturday.

An agency's risk is not volume, it is crossed wires: the wrong diary, the wrong question set, a sales script read to a lettings applicant, a viewing offered on a flat that went under offer at four o'clock. Each branch and each listing is its own object inside one account.

01Its own stock and its own diary

Availability, price and the date a property is free come from that branch's own listings, and a slot is only offered in that branch's own diary. Nothing is offered on a listing marked under offer.

02Its own approved question set

Sales and lettings ask different things, and so do new homes. The set is held per listing type, and within a type it is one fixed sequence — not a per-caller judgement call.

03Each negotiator sees only their own

A negotiator opens the board and sees their applicants, their viewings, their refused questions — not the whole agency's, and not another branch's vendors.

04You see all of them, side by side

One report, every branch in the same row: enquiries taken, viewings booked, appraisals booked, questions the AI refused, and the median time from enquiry to a slot in a diary.

FAQ

Agencies like yours ask us

A portal enquiry comes in at nine at night. What actually happens?

It answers, confirms the listing against your own availability and advertised price, then asks the applicant the questions your agency approved, in the order your agency approved them. If a slot exists that both the negotiator's diary and the occupier's notice period allow, the viewing is booked on that call and confirmed in writing. If no slot fits, it says so rather than inventing one.

Will it tell a caller what a property is worth?

No, in any wording. It gives no valuation, no estimate, no range and no opinion on whether an asking price is high or low. A price question turns into a market appraisal booked into a named valuer's diary, which is the outcome the agency actually wants from that call.

Will it tell an applicant what offer the vendor would accept?

Never. It does not say what will be accepted, does not hint at a figure, and does not disclose another party's offer or another party's circumstances. The question is recorded word for word against the applicant and put in front of the negotiator who is allowed to answer it.

How do you stop it steering people toward or away from a property?

By making the script identical. Every caller on a given listing gets the same questions in the same order, from a set you approve before it goes live, and the transcript shows that it did. It answers nothing about who lives in an area or what kind of person a street suits, and it never varies what it offers based on anything about the caller.

Do you plug into Rightmove, Zoopla or our agency software?

Not with a direct API, and we will not claim one. Portal enquiries reach the AI the way they already reach you — the forwarded call or the forwarded email — and applicant records leave through Zapier, a direct feed or CSV export. Direct agency-software integrations are in build and the founding cohort votes on the order.

Can it book a market appraisal, not just a viewing?

Yes, and that is the call worth the most to the office. It takes the address, the reason for moving if the caller offers it, and the times that suit, then books into the diary of a valuer you nominate. It does not give a figure on the phone to warm the lead up, because that is the one thing that would cost you the instruction.

Can I start with evenings and weekends only?

That is how most agencies start. Forward the line after 6 PM, all Saturday afternoon and all Sunday, read a week of transcripts, then widen the window once the answers sound like your office. Turning it off again is a change in your own phone settings, not a support ticket.

Why a fixed script is the feature

A person has a good day
and a bad day. A script
has neither.

Housing and equality law binds an agent's front line, and the wording differs by country. In the United States the Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. §3604, enforced by HUD) makes it unlawful to refuse to show or rent a dwelling, or to state a preference, because of race, color, religion, sex, familial status, national origin or disability. In Great Britain the Equality Act 2010 applies its own list of protected characteristics to the disposal and management of premises. Other countries have their own regimes and we do not claim one worldwide standard.

What every version of it has in common is the question asked afterwards: was this caller treated the same as the last one? That is a hard question to answer about four negotiators, three of whom were busy and one of whom was on a mobile in a car park. It is an easy one to answer about a fixed question set, asked in a fixed order, with a transcript for each call. That is the part of this product an agency principal should actually be buying.

The same logic sets the money boundaries. In the United Kingdom, advising on a regulated mortgage contract is an activity regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority, and what a letting agent in England may charge a tenant is set by the Tenant Fees Act 2019 — which is exactly why the AI reads your published fee list back rather than explaining what the law permits.

Two things this does not mean. It is not legal advice, and Saleringo does not assess your compliance — your agency and its advisers do that. And a consistent script is not a defence on its own: a question set that should never have been approved is still a problem, asked consistently. You write the questions. We make sure everyone gets the same ones.
