A solo practice does not have an intake team. It has whoever is not in court. The call that comes at 11:20 PM is the same call you would have taken carefully at eleven in the morning — and there is no reason it should be answered any less carefully because of the hour.

A nine-to-six practice is closed for 15 of the day’s 24 hours — and both weekend days after that. Across a full week the line is staffed for 45 of 168 hours. A person served with proceedings on a Friday afternoon spends the weekend inside the other 123.

Nobody was surveyed for that. Nine hours a day times five days is where the forty-five comes from.

The only gate in the product

Every other trade books first.
This one checks first.

In a clinic or a garage the calendar is the point. In a law firm the calendar is downstream of a question that has to be answered first: may this firm act at all? So the conflict check is not a box on an intake form here. It is wired into the calendar, and until it clears there is no slot to offer.

01The matter type is checked against your practice list

An inquiry your firm does not take never reaches the diary at all. It becomes a referral, with the reason attached, instead of a consultation nobody wanted.

02The opposing party is taken, by name

Plus any related names the caller mentions. This is the field the whole gate runs on, and it is asked for before anything is offered.

03Nothing may be scheduled until it clears

The AI runs the names against your firm's own conflicts list. It does not exercise the judgement — a person clears or declines it. Until then the calendar has nothing to show.

04If it does not clear, it says so and stops

"We're not able to act on this" — and nothing more. It never explains why, because the reason belongs to somebody else. Your referral list goes out instead.

The work it readies

  • The conflict check — queued the moment the names are taken; a solicitor runs it first thing, and the held slot is confirmed or released on their decision.
  • The urgent pile — flagged matters sort first on Monday morning, with the account and the dates verbatim, before anything else is read.
  • The declined matter — one sentence, no reason, the hold released and the firm's referral list sent the same morning.
  • The engagement letter — cost sensitivity is noted on the matter, so the solicitor who has seen the papers leads with the total.

What stays a human decision

  • The merits. Whether somebody has a case is a solicitor's judgement on the papers — never opined on, not even as a hedge.
  • Deadlines. No limitation period or filing date is ever stated. The dates are recorded as given and a solicitor confirms the one that applies.
  • The conflict decision. The AI runs the names; a person clears or declines — and a declined check is never explained.

Where it stops

What it will never do.

It takes the matter. It never advises on it.

Does the AI give legal advice?

No — a flat rule, not a score the model has to clear. It does not opine on the merits, state a limitation period or filing deadline, estimate what a matter is worth, or predict an outcome, even as a hedge.

On the call it states, in your firm's approved wording, that no lawyer–client relationship is formed. It captures the account word for word, books the consultation once the conflict check clears, and puts the thread in front of a solicitor.

How does the conflict check work if a machine is answering?

The AI collects the opposing party and related names and runs them against your firm's own conflicts list. A person clears or declines the check — the AI exercises no judgement. Until it clears, nothing may be scheduled: the gate sits on the calendar itself. If it does not clear, the AI says only that the firm is not able to act, offers your referral list, and never explains why.

Are records from different matters kept apart?

Yes. Matter records are walled from each other, not pooled in one shared list. A record opened on one matter is not visible from another, and access is set per matter during onboarding. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest, kept separate from every other firm, with retention and deletion controls your firm operates — see Security & data.

How does this sit with bar advertising rules?

Most jurisdictions prohibit outcome claims, guarantees and unqualified specialisation claims to a prospective client. The never-list on this page maps to those prohibitions one for one: no merits opinion, no predicted outcome, no case value, no superiority claim.
