The fields a clinic actually needs

A generic CRM asks for a name.
This one asks which language.

  • →Multilingual intake. The intake questions go out in the language the patient wrote in, and the answers land on one record your desk reads in yours.
  • →Referral routing. A request you do not treat becomes a referral card with the reason attached, instead of a dead end and a lost patient.
  • →Recalls that set themselves. The follow-up interval starts the day the patient is seen — in their language, on the channel they used.
  • →Symptom category, never a diagnosis. The record says what area the patient asked about. It does not say what is wrong with them, and it never will.

The $120 assessment, the shift list,
and the question it never answers.

A clinic record is worth little without the reason for the visit in the patient’s own words. Self-pay or insured is a field too, not a note. The calendar will not hold a treatment slot until a consultation has been attended — the $120 buys the assessment, not the procedure.

Same-day offers come only from practitioners actually on shift. A quote is that fee plus a band from your price list, with insurer coverage always stated as unconfirmed. And nothing here will answer a symptom, judge urgency, or say one word about medication.

What the calendar enforces
  1. A consultation has to be attended before a treatment slot can be held — the $120 buys the assessment, not the procedure.
  2. Same-day requests are offered only from the practitioners actually on shift that day, never the standing timetable.
  3. Follow-ups and package sessions are offered against the practitioner the patient actually saw.
What a quote is built from
  • Consultation fee — $120, credited against treatment
  • Procedure band from your price list
  • Sessions in the package, and the package price
  • Insurer coverage — always stated as unconfirmed
Who ends up with the work

Reception confirms coverage and gets the consent form signed before a treatment slot is final.

Where it stops

Symptoms, triage, medication, whether it is urgent. Every one of those is a person.

Room availability, slot length and pricing all come from your own setup. Those are the numbers a practice manager already watches — the only change is who was awake when the call came in.
