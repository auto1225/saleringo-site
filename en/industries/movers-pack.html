The fields a removals firm actually needs

A generic CRM asks for a name.
This one asks whether there is a lift.

  • →Both ends, every time. Pickup and destination postcode, property type, bedrooms, floor, lift, and the carry from the door to where the van can actually stand.
  • →The date, and what fixes it. A completion date is not a preference. The record says which it is, because that decides whether you hold the slot.
  • →Awkward items by name. Piano, safe, American fridge, gym rig, ride-on mower. Written down and put to a person — never agreed to on the phone.
  • →A Monday number. Enquiries taken, surveys booked, survey-to-booking rate, storage weeks live — exportable to PDF or Excel.
See the pipeline it fills

Floor, lift and carry —
and the move it will not price.

A removals record needs both postcodes before a single word about price, then the four facts that set the crew: property, bedrooms, floor and lift. Parking, carry distance and awkward items by name — piano, safe, American fridge — are fields, because they are what turns a two-man day into a three-man one. Where your rules say a job needs a survey, the call books a survey rather than inventing a number. Dates come off the diary and nothing is held on goodwill. Completion-day timing is never guaranteed — waiting time is read from the rate card and the decision goes to a person. And nothing here prices a move nobody has surveyed.

What the diary enforces
  1. A price request that your rules say needs a survey books a survey, not a number.
  2. Dates come off the diary. Nothing is held on goodwill, and no crew is named that is booked elsewhere.
  3. Completion-day timing is never guaranteed — waiting time is quoted from the rate card and the decision goes to a person.
What a quote is built from
  • Volume and crew hours from the survey
  • Access — floors, lift, carry, bay suspension
  • Packing and materials, priced as chosen
  • Storage by container-week, and the cover level taken
Who ends up with the work

Surveyor takes the video or in-person survey; the move manager builds the quote from the card.

Where it stops

Pricing a move nobody has surveyed, or promising an hour that belongs to somebody else's chain.
