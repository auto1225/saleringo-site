The fields this trade actually needs

A generic CRM asks for a name.
This one asks how high it is.

  • →A species field that can be left empty. Most systems force a category to save the record. This one saves unconfirmed, and the technician's identification is what closes it.
  • →Who is at risk, as a field. Sting history, infants, pets, fish, hives nearby — captured as facts stated by the caller, never as a safety judgment made on the phone.
  • →Position, height and access. Whether the van needs the triple ladder, and whether there is a flat roof to get on to, decided before it leaves the yard.
  • →Programmes, not one-offs. Visit 2 of 3 falls due on its own and gets offered on its own, with the same technician and the same gate code.
  • →Commercial reports, dated. Which sites are owed a written report, and which have had one — the column an inspection turns into a problem.
See the pipeline it fills

The caller’s words, the right ladder,
and the species it will not name.

The pest goes into the record in the caller’s own words and stays unconfirmed until a technician sees it. Domestic or food business decides the card and the paperwork; position, height and access decide which ladder leaves the yard. Who is at risk — sting history, infants, pets, fish, hives — is asked before anything is booked.

Bedbugs and commercial rodent work are surveyed before they are priced. And it will not name a species, judge a sting, or call a product safe around a particular child, animal or condition.

What the diary enforces
  1. A same-day slot is offered only when one exists — the diary answers, not the caller's urgency.
  2. Bedbugs and commercial rodent work are surveyed before they are priced, every time.
  3. Programme follow-ups fall due at your interval and are offered without anyone remembering them.
What a quote is built from
  • Nest price, and the second-nest price
  • Out-of-hours or early slot, if the slot exists
  • Programme price — all visits in, follow-ups included
  • Survey fee, credited or not, exactly as you set it
Who ends up with the work

The technician confirms the species on site and decides what is used in that room.

Where it stops

Naming a species, judging a sting, or telling anyone a product is safe around a particular child, animal or condition.

What you judge it on after ninety days
Evening and weekend calls capturedSurveys converted to treatmentProgramme follow-ups attendedCommercial reports issued on time
