Where the calendar says no

A patch test is not
a preference. It is a gate.

Oxidative colour carries an allergy-alert test — for most salons 48 hours ahead — because the manufacturer's own instructions say so, and because professional indemnity insurers routinely ask whether you follow them. So this is not a script the AI reads. It is a rule the calendar enforces: inside the window, no colour date exists to offer. It ships enabled, and it is not a setting anyone can switch off to hit a booking target.

Booking refused "Can I skip the patch test?"

The booking stops. Your own rule is read word for word, no colour date inside the window is offered in any wording, and the first clear date is put on the table instead. The request itself goes on the record.

Routed to a stylist "I reacted a bit last time"

A reported reaction is never assessed, minimised or explained away. The AI records the client's own words, refuses to book colour at all, and puts a named stylist on it. Skin, scalp and allergy questions are a permanent handoff.

Hard boundary No promised result, no sight-unseen price

It will not say hair will lift to a photograph, that extensions will look like an image, or that a correction is one sitting. Corrections, extensions and repairs get the consultation fee and a consultation — never a number.

STEP 01Ask, alwaysFirst colour here, or a test older than your window? The question is asked before a date is ever mentioned.
STEP 02Block the datesThe calendar removes every colour slot inside the window. There is nothing to offer, so nothing gets offered.
STEP 03Book the test firstThe patch test is booked as its own short appointment, and the colour date is set from it — not the other way round.
STEP 04Record the declarationDate, who took it, and anything the client said about a past reaction, verbatim, on the client record.

Second rule, same shape: many salons require a guardian present for under-16 colour. The AI applies your policy as written and never interprets it — it asks the age, states the rule, and routes anything that does not fit to a person. How answers get approved →

Two salons under one owner almost never run the same menu, the same columns or the same deposit. Each address keeps its own menu, its own columns and its own deposit rule, and the AI never reads across the two.

A client asking about The Studio hears The Studio's bands, its own surcharges and its own closed day. Never the other shop's prices, in either direction.

FAQ

Salons like yours ask us

Someone asks to skip the patch test. What exactly happens?

The booking stops. The AI reads your own patch-test rule word for word, refuses to offer any date inside the window, and offers the first date that clears it. If the caller mentions a previous reaction it does not assess it — it routes to a stylist and puts the caller's own words on the record. This is not a setting anyone can switch off to hit a booking target.

Can it quote colour over the phone?

It quotes the band on your service menu, and it names the length or condition surcharge out loud so the number is not a surprise at the chair. What it will not do is put a single figure on a colour correction, an extension set or a repair that nobody has looked at — that gets the consultation fee and a consultation.

Does it book into the right stylist's column?

Yes. A salon has one calendar per column, not one calendar. If a client asks for a named stylist the AI books that column, holds the number of consecutive slots the service actually needs, and never puts a double-slot service into a single gap on somebody else's day.

Does it take deposits?

It states your deposit rule and sends your own payment link. It never takes a card number over the phone or in a chat, and until the deposit clears the slot is shown as held, not confirmed — on the record and to the client.

Can I start with evenings and Sundays only?

That is how most salons begin. Forward the line once the last column is finished and over the closed day, read the transcripts for a week, then widen the window when the answers sound like your salon. Turning it off again is one change to your call forwarding, made by you, in a minute.
