The line the whole page rests on

It will not price a repair
before the car is on the ramp.

Not as a range. Not as a "usually around". Not if the caller reads out a fault code, and not if they push three times. It quotes the diagnostic fee and your published hourly labour rate, and it stops. The repair gets quoted when somebody has looked at the car, and the customer approves it before anything is touched.

If that sentence sounds like it will cost you bookings: it is the same sentence that stops a $180 phone guess turning into a $640 argument on the forecourt, and it is the reason this can go on your main line at all. It ships enabled. It is not a setting anybody can switch off to hit a booking target.

Refused outright "What's that going to cost?"

No repair price, sight unseen, in any wording. The diagnostic fee and the labour rate are quoted; the ramp is booked; the caller's own description of the fault goes on the job card verbatim, so the technician reads what she actually said rather than a paraphrase.

Refused outright "Is it safe for me to drive it in?"

Roadworthiness on an unseen vehicle is never judged. The AI reads your approved wording back, offers recovery and quotes the recovery fee, and pages the owner if the description carries a safety keyword — brakes, steering, smoke, a fuel smell.

Hard boundary No diagnosis from a code

A fault code is a symptom, not a diagnosis, and the difference between the two is the difference between a job and a comeback. The code is written down exactly as read out, and the ramp time is booked to find out what it means.

STEP 01Quote what is knowableDiagnostic fee, labour rate, safety inspection, recovery. Four numbers that exist before anyone looks at the car.
STEP 02Write down the restSymptom in the caller's own words, when it happens, codes as read out. Captured, never interpreted.
STEP 03Agree the ceilingThe figure under which the shop may work without ringing back, taken on the call and written on the job card.
STEP 04Leave a trailRecording, transcript, the ceiling and the caller's words land on the job card before the car arrives.

Why the ceiling sits on the call, not on the invoice. Most jurisdictions require written authorisation before work begins, and a fresh authorisation before an estimate is exceeded — US state auto-repair acts, and the equivalent consumer-contract rules across the EU and UK. Taking the ceiling at 6:52 PM means the authorisation exists before the ramp rather than being chased after the bill. Two more, in the same spirit. Your own recording notice is played at the start of every call, in your wording, because the job card is only worth something if the conversation behind it can be produced. And on collision work the AI never speaks for an insurer and never states what a policy covers. Recording-consent and repair-authorisation rules differ by country and by state, and we set the wording with you rather than shipping a default. How answers get approved →

Two sites. Three. A group.

The body shop does not quote
the service shop's labour rate.

A mechanical site and a body shop almost never share a rate card, a ramp count or an authorisation ceiling. The two run as separate shops behind one login, each with its own board, and neither is ever quoted from the other’s rates.

01Its own number, hours and rate card

A caller reaching the body shop hears the body shop's labour rate and the body shop's hours. Never the other site's numbers, in either direction.

02Its own ramps and its own loaner fleet

The job is booked onto a ramp that actually exists at that site, and a courtesy car is held from the fleet parked there — not from a group total.

03Its own authorisation ceiling

A $250 default at one site and a $500 default at another is normal, and the ceiling read out on the call is always the one belonging to the site the car is going to.

FAQ

Shops like yours ask us

Will it quote a repair over the phone?

No. It quotes the diagnostic fee and your published hourly labour rate, and nothing else. The repair gets quoted when the car is on the ramp and somebody has looked at it, and the customer approves it before anything is touched. That refusal ships enabled and is not a setting anyone can switch off to hit a booking target.

What is the authorisation ceiling, and why is it on the call?

It is the figure under which your team may work without ringing the customer back, agreed on the call and written on the job card. Most jurisdictions require written authorisation before work begins and a fresh authorisation before exceeding an estimate. Capturing the ceiling on the call means the authorisation exists before the ramp instead of being chased after the invoice.

Does it ask for the VIN or the plate?

It asks for the plate first, because people know it, and the VIN when the plate is not enough. That is what turns a guess into a part number: year, make, model and engine give you a labour time from the guide; the VIN gives you the right part. The AI captures it — your parts desk still places the order.

Someone asks whether their car is safe to drive in. What happens?

It refuses, in every wording. It reads your approved wording back, offers recovery and quotes the recovery fee. If the description contains a safety keyword — brakes, steering, smoke, a fuel smell — it pages the owner rather than deciding on its own that a described symptom is probably fine.

Is every call recorded?

Yes, and your own recording notice is played at the start of the call in your own wording — because the whole point of the job card is that the authorisation and the customer's own description of the fault can be produced later. Recording-consent law varies by country and by state; we set the notice with you during onboarding rather than shipping a default.
