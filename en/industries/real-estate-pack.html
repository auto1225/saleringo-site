The fields a front desk actually needs

A generic CRM asks for a name.
This one asks when their tenancy ends.

  • →The approved question set, in order. Not a checklist the AI works through in whatever order the conversation wanders — a fixed sequence you sign off, identical for every caller, and visible as such in the transcript. It is the field that makes the rest of this page defensible.
  • →Position to proceed — captured, never scored. Chain-free, first-time buyer, mortgage in principle held or not, when the current tenancy ends. Written down as the caller said it. The AI does not rank applicants and does not decide who gets offered which slot.
  • →What was said about money, word for word. Holding deposits, referencing criteria and what a tenant can be charged are the first thing an applicant disputes later. The record shows what was stated, when, and to whom.
  • →Everything it refused. A separate field, not a line lost in a transcript — because "would they take less" is the call you most want a human ringing back about, and it should be the first thing on the board.
See the pipeline it fills

The same questions in the same order —
and not one word about price.

Property management books a repair behind a tenant's door. This pack books a stranger into somebody's home on a Saturday morning, at a price it is forbidden to discuss. That is why the approved question set exists here and nowhere else in the product — and why nothing in this pack has a spend limit.

What the record carries

The approved question setthe same questions, in the same order, for everyone
Position to proceedchain, funding, tenancy end date — captured, never scored
Fee facts as statedreferencing criteria and deposits, in your published words
Everything it refusedthe price question, verbatim, in its own field
What the diary enforces
  1. The approved questions are asked in order, for every caller — never reordered to suit a conversation.
  2. No price, no valuation, no view on an offer; a market appraisal is booked instead.
  3. A slot is only offered where the negotiator's diary and the occupier's notice period both allow it.
What a viewing is built from
  • Accompanied, or by arrangement with the occupier
  • The notice the sitting tenant was promised
  • The negotiator's own diary, not a shared pool
  • The fee facts, stated before the viewing rather than after
Who ends up with the work

The negotiator takes the viewing and the offer; the valuer owns anything to do with price.

Where it stops

Valuing, negotiating, or saying anything that sorts callers by who they are.
