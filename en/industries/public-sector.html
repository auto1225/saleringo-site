For councils, municipal offices, utilities & public agencies · the main line

The main burst at 1:40 AM.
One call woke the duty officer. Nine got a reference number.

A public switchboard at night is two jobs at once. One caller is standing next to water coming up through the road and has to reach a person tonight. The other nine want a bin day, an opening time, a form, or a fee — and need to be answered, logged and given a reference so they stop ringing back. Saleringo does both, quotes only what your authority published, and makes no determination of any kind.

The expensive thing about a public switchboard is not the hard calls. It is that the same simple question arrives three times through three channels because the first two attempts went into a queue and the caller gave up. Answer it once, log it once, give a reference — and it stops coming back.

See how it answers out of hours  →

A nine-to-five office is closed for 16 of the day’s 24 hours — and both weekend days after that. Across a full week the main line is staffed for 40 of 168 hours. A main that goes at 5:05 on a Friday is inside the other 128.

This is not a statistic we collected. It is what an ordinary working week divides into.

The one distinction the whole pack is built on

Every other trade books first.
This one decides nothing.

In a clinic or a garage the calendar is the point. A public main line has nothing to book. It takes three kinds of call — one that must reach a person tonight, one with a published answer, and one that asks software to decide what only your authority may decide. The pack keeps those three apart.

01The question is matched to something you published

Collection day, opening hours, how to pay, which form, what to bring, what a fee is. If your authority has not published it, the AI does not have it — and says so rather than reaching for the general answer.

02Out of hours, it triages against your own list

Your authority names the categories that wake a duty officer tonight and the wording that goes with each. A call it cannot match to a named category goes to your on-call contact anyway. It does not invent categories.

03It never makes a determination

Not eligibility, not an entitlement, not a planning or enforcement outcome, not what a rule means for this person. No hedge either — "usually" and "normally" are determinations wearing a coat.

04Everything else is logged, with a reference and a service

A reference number read out before the call ends, the service area attached, the caller told plainly that it is a next-working-day item and that nothing has been decided.

Where it stops

What it will never do.

In a public body, the answers software refuses to give matter more than the ones it gives. These boundaries are built in — not configured, not optional, and they do not soften because the caller is angry, upset, or asking for the third time.

It logs, routes and quotes. It never decides.
✕ Decide eligibility or confirm an entitlementNot “you should qualify”, not “that normally applies from the date they left”, and not a hedge that implies either. It records the question in the caller’s own words and routes it to the service that decides.
✕ Give a planning, licensing or enforcement outcomeNo prediction, no likelihood, no “that usually gets refused”. It gives the published register entry, the consultation route and the named case officer, and stops there.
✕ Interpret a policy, a regulation or a lawReading a rule and applying it to one person’s facts is a decision, not information. The published wording may be sent; what it means for this caller is a person’s answer.
✕ Say anything that implies a decision has been madeEvery logged contact carries the sentence out loud: no decision has been made. A reference number is a receipt for a question, and the AI never lets it sound like an outcome.
✕ Answer from anything your authority has not publishedNo general knowledge, no neighbouring authority’s rules, no last year’s fee. If it is not in the approved set, it is a handoff — and the record says which page every answer came from.
✕ Promise a visit, a response time or a crewIt states the service standard your authority published, if there is one, and otherwise says plainly that it does not know. An invented “someone will be out tonight” is the call you hear about in the complaint.
✕ Invent an out-of-hours category to seem helpfulThe list belongs to your authority. A call it cannot match is escalated to the on-call contact you nominated, flagged as unmatched, rather than quietly filed until Monday.

Three things it says out loud

The disclaimers are not in the footer.
They are in the call.

Two of these are said on the call itself, in your authority's own approved wording, rather than buried where nobody reads them. The third is a property of which channel the caller is on.

“No decision has been made.”Said out loud on every contact that touches an entitlement, an application or a complaint. A reference number is a receipt for a question, never an outcome, and the AI does not let it sound like one.
“Your reference is 4471-B.”Issued in your own format, read out before the call ends and repeated in writing on the channel they used. It is the single cheapest thing you can give somebody who would otherwise ring back twice.
The phone is the accessible channelIt answers in the language the call arrives in, records that language, and stores large print, post-only or “no email” as an enforced rule rather than a note somebody has to read.

Language and access on a public line are often a written duty, not a courtesy. Welsh language standards under the Welsh Language (Wales) Measure 2011 require councils to greet main-number callers in Welsh and to handle the call in Welsh if the caller wishes.

In the United States, Executive Order 13166 (2000) requires federal agencies and recipients of federal funds to provide meaningful access for people with limited English proficiency.

Directive (EU) 2016/2102, applied since 23 September 2018, covers public bodies' websites and apps — so the phone must still work for the people a website cannot reach. Which duties bind your authority is for you and your regulator; nothing on this page is legal advice.

Questions service managers ask

Asked by authorities like yours.

Can the AI decide whether somebody is eligible for something?

No — the refusal is categorical, not a confidence threshold. It never decides eligibility, confirms an entitlement, interprets a policy, or predicts a planning or enforcement outcome. Not even as a hedge, and not even where the general rule is published.

It says on the call that no decision has been made, records the question in the caller's own words, gives a reference number, and routes the contact to the service that decides.

How does it know what counts as an emergency at two in the morning?

From your own out-of-hours list, not ours. Your authority names the categories that reach a duty officer tonight — a burst main, a dangerous structure, a tree blocking a carriageway — and the wording for each.

The AI matches the call against that list and nothing else. It never invents a category: a call it cannot match goes to the on-call contact you nominated, flagged as unmatched, and the caller is told so.

Can it answer callers who do not speak the majority language?

It holds the call in the language it arrives in, and records that language so the callback is not made in the wrong one. The published-source rule does not relax in translation: a page published in one language only is answered by saying so and routing to a person.

What does the audit trail actually hold?

Who contacted you, on which channel, at what time; what they asked in their own words and what was said back; which published page the answer came from and when you last approved it; the reference issued and where the contact was routed.

Refusals are recorded as fully as answers — the question the AI would not touch is the one most likely to be asked about later. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest, kept separate from every other customer, with retention and deletion controls your authority operates — see Security & data.
