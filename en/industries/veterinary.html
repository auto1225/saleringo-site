What actually arrives, and when

Half of it is a booster.
The other half is a person
who should not be talking to software.

No other trade on this site has a sharper line through its inbox. On one side, the questions every practice hears over and over — hours, boosters, nail clips, what a dental costs, whether you see rabbits.

On the other, toxicity, dosage, symptoms and end-of-life, where a confident machine answer is worse than none. A veterinary AI belongs on your main line only if it tells the two apart every time, out loud, in the first sentence.

✕The 9 PM call you never hear aboutThe owner rings, gets voicemail, rings the emergency hospital instead — and the practice finds out days later, if at all.
✕The booster that quietly lapsesVaccination and post-op recalls are the most reliable recurring work in the building, and they leak every week the desk is busy.
✕The price shopper at 6:50 PMThey are ringing four practices in one evening. Whoever answers with a real, weight-banded figure books the animal.

A practice open 8:30 to 6:30 on weekdays and Saturday morning is staffed for 53 hours — fifty across the week, three on the Saturday. That is 53 of 168 hours. An animal that stops eating does not wait for Monday; the owner in the headline above rang inside the other 115.

That is your own opening board multiplied out, not a number anybody sold us.

Two branches, a night partner, one number

Westfield does not quote
Riverside's price sheet.

Most practices are not one building. There is the main surgery, the branch that took over somebody's retirement, the yard round, and the hospital that covers the night. Each of those runs on its own rota and its own consult list behind a single practice login — and the AI is only ever allowed to name the partners you entered yourself.

01Its own number, hours and price sheet

An owner asking about Westfield hears Westfield's hours, Westfield's clinicians and Westfield's bands. Never the other site's figures, in either direction.

02Its own list, its own recalls

Bookings land on the calendar of the branch the owner actually rang, with a clinician who sees that species. Booster and post-op recalls run per site.

03Referral and out-of-hours partners are entered, not inferred

The night hospital, the imaging centre, the specialist you refer orthopaedics to — each is a named destination with its own instructions. The AI cannot invent one, and it will say it does not know rather than guess.

Your Westfield manager opens the pipeline and sees Westfield. You open one report with every site in the same row: inquiries, bookings out of hours, recalls answered, conversations routed to a person.

FAQ

Veterinary practices ask us

Will it ever give clinical advice?

No, and not as a setting you could switch on. Toxicity, dosage, symptom assessment, "should I be worried", medication and end-of-life questions are refused categorically. The AI says so in the first sentence, routes the caller to the number you nominated, and writes the record so your team is not starting cold. It books, quotes and reminds. It does not assess.

Does it connect to our practice-management system?

Not directly — whichever one you run, treat it as a bridge. Records reach it through Zapier or a CSV export your desk imports, and every conversation also lands in Saleringo's own inbox with bookings on its own scheduler. A bridge means somebody does a step. Be honest about whether your desk will do that daily — if not, this is not ready for you yet.

What happens with an out-of-hours emergency?

It stops answering questions. The caller gets the out-of-hours instructions and the number you nominated — your own on-call line, or the partner hospital you entered — in the first sentence. Your staff are alerted and a record created, so the follow-up actually happens. It never handles an emergency alone and never tries to judge how urgent something is.

Can it quote prices?

Only figures your vets have approved, and for anaesthetic work only as a weight-banded range, confirmed after the animal has been examined. If a price is not on the approved list, the AI says it cannot quote it and books the appointment that produces the real figure. Nothing is ever improvised, rounded or averaged.

Can it see the animal's record — boosters, weight, history?

It works from the record held in Saleringo — built from the conversations themselves and anything you import at onboarding. That is what lets it read a booster due date instead of asking for it. Where your patient software is the source of truth, the bridge above keeps the two aligned — we would rather you knew that up front.

Can it handle repeat prescriptions?

It can take the request, attach the record and put it in front of a vet immediately, with the detail that matters — how much medication is left — captured. It cannot name a drug, confirm a quantity, suggest timing or authorise anything. Repeats are a veterinary decision and they stay one.

We run two branches and refer to a hospital. How does routing work?

Each branch is its own site inside one account, with its own number, hours, clinicians, species list and price sheet. Referral and out-of-hours partners are entered by you as named destinations with their own instructions. The AI cannot invent a partner and will say it does not know rather than guess — see branches and referral partners above.

What does it cost?

The published plans: Start $79/mo, Grow $249/mo, Scale from $599/mo. AI Phone starts at Scale, and voice usage is billed on top from $0.14/min in the US. Locations and channels are separate limits, which catches people out, so the pricing page works your month through in full. Founding cohort is 50% off for three months.
