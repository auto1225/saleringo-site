The Franchise & multi-site pack

Everything in the pack,
and what it will not do.

The CRM behind the call, the fields and stages this trade actually works in, and the systems it hands to. The argument for any of it is on the Franchise & multi-site page.

Every other pack has one pipeline.
This trade has two, and they must never touch.

Customer bookings move along one board, per location, visible to that location. Franchise candidates move along a second board that lives at head office and that no store manager can open. The duality is the whole trade — so it is drawn as two boards, not one board with a filter.

Why two boards and not one

A filter can be removed.
A separate pipeline cannot.

  • →Location is resolved before anything is quoted. The AI does not answer a price question until it knows which site it is answering for — and if the caller is ambiguous, it asks rather than picks.
  • →Candidates never appear in a store's inbox. Your franchisees do not need to know who is asking about a territory near them, and in most groups it is actively unhelpful that they do.
  • →The refusals are structural, not tone. Earnings, territory availability and contract terms are not phrased carefully — they are not answered, and the candidate is handed to your franchise team with the question intact.
  • →One report, every site in the same row. Inquiries, handled without staff, booked, missed during opening hours — per location and combined, exportable to PDF or Excel for the Monday meeting.
See the pipeline it fills

One customer record,
twenty diaries that stay separate.

A group answering service fails in one particular way: it treats twenty sites as one. So every field here names the site — which location, that site’s hours, that site’s staff. A booking is held against that site’s own diary, never a group average, and prices are read from the list the franchisee filed. The AI never commits a calendar that belongs to somebody else.

What the record carries
Customer name and numberone record across every site
Which locationor the nearest one
Service requestedfrom the group catalogue
Preferred timeagainst that site’s hours
How they found the groupattribution rolls up
New or existingdecides the routing
What the calendar enforces
  1. A price only exists once a location has been resolved. Prices are held per site, never per group, and there is no group default to fall back on.
  2. A booking is held against that site’s own calendar and staff, not a group average.
  3. A record belongs to the site that took it. Another site’s manager cannot open it; only the group report sees both rows.
What a quote is built from
  • Group price for the service
  • Site-level adjustment, if approved
  • Any local promotion in force
  • Total, with the site named on the document
Who ends up with the work

The site manager owns the booking — and head office can see it.

Where it stops

Approving a discount outside group policy, or committing another site’s calendar.

What you judge it on after ninety days
Inquiries answered per site after hoursSite-level conversion, comparedGroup revenue by sourceSites below the group answer rate

Each site’s hours, staff and price list are read from that site’s own record, never a group average. The screen is drawn; the object model is real.Inquiries answered per site, conversion compared site by site, one group view over both. Until every site answers, you cannot tell a weak location from an unanswered phone.

This is the manifest, not the argument. Why any of it matters — the call that arrives at night, what it costs to miss, what the AI refuses to say — is on the page this came from.
