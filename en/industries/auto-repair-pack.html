Six fields no generic CRM asks for

A generic CRM asks for a name.
This one asks for the plate.

  • →The authorisation ceiling, taken on the call. The single most valuable field on the card, and the one no other pack in this product has. It decides whether the morning starts with work or with a phone call nobody answers.
  • →The symptom in the caller's own words, plus when. "Only on a cold start" and "only over sixty" are two different diagnostic paths and two different amounts of ramp time to hold.
  • →Drivable, or a recovery. Not a preference — a completely different job, with a different price, a different vehicle and a different arrival time.
  • →A Monday number. Calls taken, ramps filled, evening share, diagnostic-to-authorised-work conversion, first-time fix — exportable to PDF or Excel.
See the pipeline it fills

Plate, ceiling, and the sentence
it will never say.

A workshop record is worthless without the VIN and the authorisation ceiling, so both are fields rather than notes. The calendar stops dead at that ceiling, and a safety word — brakes, steering, smoke — pages a person instead of booking a ramp. An estimate is the diagnostic fee plus your hourly rate times the published guide time; parts are priced after the ramp, never before. And nothing here will ever tell a caller their car is safe to drive.

What the record carries
Year, make, model, enginegives the guide time, gives the labour figure
Plate, then VINgives the part number, not a near-enough one
Symptom, and whencold start, braking, above sixty
Drivable or recoverya tow is a different job from a slot
Courtesy car or wait-in-shopdecides the slot length and the bay
Authorisation ceilingand who is allowed to raise it
What the calendar enforces
  1. Work stops at the authorisation ceiling. Above it nothing is touched until a recorded approval exists.
  2. A safety keyword — brakes, steering, smoke, fuel smell — stops the booking script and pages a person.
  3. A recovery is booked as a tow with its own arrival, never as a ramp slot the car cannot reach.
What a quote is built from
  • Diagnostic fee, and whether it comes off the repair
  • Hourly labour rate × the published guide time
  • Recovery, safety inspection, courtesy car — the knowable four
  • Parts — quoted after the ramp, never before
Who ends up with the work

The workshop controller assigns the ramp and writes the estimate; the customer authorises it before a spanner moves.

Where it stops

Pricing a repair before the car is on the ramp, diagnosing from a description or a code, and saying a car is safe to drive.

Ramp availability, guide times, ceilings and pricing all come from your own setup. The screen is drawn; the six fields and three rules underneath it are the shipping ones.Ramps booked while you were shut, how many diagnostics became authorised work, and whether comebacks moved.

This is the manifest, not the argument. Why any of it matters — the call that arrives at night, what it costs to miss, what the AI refuses to say — is on the page this came from.
