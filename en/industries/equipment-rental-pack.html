The fields a hire desk actually needs

A generic CRM asks for a name.
This one asks how wide the gate is.

  • →Availability against a date range and a depot. Not "we have those" — free, at that branch, across those dates, reserved so the desk can turn it into a contract.
  • →The whole rate in one answer. Day, weekend, week and long hire, transport both ways, fuel, consumables and waiver. A caller told only the day rate feels lied to at the counter.
  • →Site facts before the wrong machine is loaded. Access width, ground, working height, indoor or outdoor, what power is on site — asked at 06:40, not discovered by a driver at 08:30.
  • →Reserved, never on hire. The AI can quote and reserve. Putting a machine on hire is your desk's decision, every time.

Date on, date off,
and the machine it will not promise.

A hire sheet starts with the date on and the date off — those two decide the rate. Gate width, ground and working height are fields; nothing wider than the access is offered. A quote is the period rate plus transport, fuel, waiver, deposit and tax. It never confirms a machine the yard has not got free, or claims a caller is trained.

What the rate card enforces
  1. A machine wider than the access given is never offered.
  2. The week or weekend rate is quoted whenever it beats the day rate across those dates — including when the customer only asked for the day rate.
  3. Transport is quoted both ways or not at all, and never folded silently into the hire rate.
What a quote is built from
  • Day, weekend, week or long-hire rate × period
  • Delivery and collection, each way
  • Fuel, consumables and damage waiver
  • Deposit, and the total with tax
Who ends up with the work

The transport desk gets the delivery and the ground condition; the fitter gets the fault and the unit number; the yard gets the pick list before the lorry loads.

Where it stops

Confirming a machine the system has not got free, or telling anyone they are qualified to run one.
