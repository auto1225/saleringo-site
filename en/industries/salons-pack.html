The fields a salon actually needs

A generic CRM asks for a name.
This one asks how long her hair is.

  • →The band, not a flat number. Length and condition decide which band inside the service, and the long-hair surcharge is real money. A flat number quoted at 9:35 PM is an argument at the chair on Saturday.
  • →The column, not the calendar. A named stylist means a named diary, and a double slot means two consecutive gaps in that diary — never one gap and a hope.
  • →The patch test as a calendar rule. Not a reminder, not a note. Inside the window, colour dates do not exist to be offered.
  • →The cancellation list, working by itself. Where a salon's lost revenue actually comes back: a 10 AM drops out, and the three people who said they would take an earlier slot hear about it in the same minute.
See the pipeline it fills

Which column, which band,
and one subject it will not discuss.

A salon record is built around a column and a band. The service sets the chair time; length, density and current colour decide the band inside it; the stylist asked for by name decides which diary is open. The patch-test date on file decides whether a colour appointment can exist at all — and the AI says out loud, every time, that skin, scalp and allergy are questions it cannot answer.

What the record carries
Service, from your menusets the band and the chair time
Length, density or current colourdecides which band inside the service
Stylist requested by namedecides which diary is booked
First visit or existing clientdecides whether a consultation comes first
Patch-test date on filedecides whether the appointment may happen at all
Deposit taken, yes or noheld versus confirmed
What the calendar enforces
  1. No colour date exists inside the patch-test window. The AI cannot offer one because there is nothing there to offer.
  2. A double-slot service takes two consecutive gaps in one column, or it is not booked.
  3. A slot stays held, not confirmed, until the deposit clears — and the client is told which it is.
What a quote is built from
  • The band on your service menu
  • Length or condition surcharge, named out loud
  • Consultation fee, where a price cannot honestly be given
  • Deposit and no-show rule, stated as written
Who ends up with the work

The stylist opens the column with the length, the history and the reference photo already on the record.

Where it stops

Promising a result, pricing a correction nobody has seen, and anything about skin, scalp or an allergy.
